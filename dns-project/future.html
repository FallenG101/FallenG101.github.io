The Future of DNS

DNS will likely keep its basic system of levels and zones. New software and standards may make it more private, faster, and easier to manage.

More private connections

Encrypted DNS options such as DoT, DoH, and DoQ can protect requests as they travel between a device and its resolver. How much privacy this provides depends on the resolver and the device or network settings.

Faster connection setup

SVCB and HTTPS records can tell a browser about other servers or supported connection methods before it connects. This can help the browser choose a good route, while the server’s TLS certificate still checks its identity.

Safer operations

Automation can check DNS changes, update DNSSEC keys, and catch mistakes before they cause outages. Good tools should make the work easier and show what they changed.

Open questions

  • How can users get privacy while schools and workplaces keep needed filters and monitoring?
  • How can people manage encrypted DNS across browsers, computers, and networks?
  • How can DNS operators prevent mistakes and keep the system easy to understand?

These questions are still being worked out. The answers will depend on standards, software, network rules, and how DNS is managed.
