The Future of DNS

DNS will likely keep its levels and zones. New software and standards may make it more private, faster, and easier to manage.

More private connections

DoT, DoH, and DoQ can protect DNS requests as they travel from a device to its resolver. The level of privacy depends on the resolver and the device or network settings.

Faster connection setup

SVCB and HTTPS records can tell a browser about other servers or connection options before it connects. This can help it choose a route. A TLS certificate still checks the server’s identity.

Safer operations

Automation can check DNS settings, update DNSSEC keys, and catch mistakes before they cause an outage. Good tools should also show what they changed.

Open questions

  • How can schools and workplaces protect privacy while keeping needed filters and monitoring?
  • How can people manage encrypted DNS across browsers, computers, and networks?
  • How can DNS operators avoid mistakes and keep DNS easy to understand?

These questions are still being worked out. The answers will depend on standards, software, network rules, and how DNS is managed.
