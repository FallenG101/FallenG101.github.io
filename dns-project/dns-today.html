DNS Today

DNS is a core part of the Internet today. Websites, email, apps, cloud services, and more use it.

What depends on DNS

  • Websites: address records tell browsers where websites are.
  • Email: MX records list the servers that receive a domain’s email.
  • Apps and cloud services: names help apps find services, even when their addresses change.
  • Content delivery: DNS can direct people to working or nearby servers.
  • Account security: TXT records can show who owns a domain and set email rules.

Security and privacy

Regular DNS requests are not encrypted. Without DNSSEC, a resolver may not know if an answer was changed. DNSSEC adds digital signatures so resolvers can check signed data. It does not hide requests or prove that a website is safe.

DNS over TLS (DoT), DNS over HTTPS (DoH), and DNS over QUIC (DoQ) encrypt the connection from a device to its resolver. The resolver can still see the names being requested. QNAME minimization shares less of a name with DNS servers along the way.

Risks to understand

  • Cache poisoning: false answers are added to a resolver’s saved data.
  • Hijacking: settings or records are changed to send people to the wrong place.
  • Amplification: open resolvers are misused to send large amounts of traffic at a target.
  • Outages: a wrong record or setting can make a service hard to reach.

DNSSEC checks that signed data has not been changed. Encrypted DNS protects the connection to the resolver. They do different things.
